Pomad Customer Review Mining

Customers describe the Cure better than any ad we have run: « Enfin un émollient que je supporte », « Je peux enfin dormir la nuit ». We read 2,652 of their reviews, kept the lines that sell, and built four ads around them. Two problems showed up too: unhappy customers judged a 3-month Cure after one month, and psoriasis customers are the hardest to satisfy.

2,652 reviews, about 70 % of all Pomad reviews on Judge.me · 78 % rated 4 or 5 stars · read on 5 Nov 2026

Two findings

What changes how we talk about the Cure

28 %of bad reviews say "no change after about a month".

The Cure is a 3-month programme, so people judge it too early. Ads that show results take time should both sell and cut disappointment. A "keep going" email at weeks 3–4 would help too.

19 % vs 9 %Psoriasis is mentioned twice as often in bad reviews as in good ones.

For psoriasis, promise comfort and time, not clear skin, and watch refunds when we push psoriasis ads.

Rules

Before a quote goes in an ad

  • Real, verified reviews only. Shorten them, never reword them. First name and initial.
  • No cure words. Some reviews say "guérison", "soigné" or "miracle", and one comes from a self-described doctor. Those lines stay out.
  • No comparison with a medicine as a headline. « Contrairement aux crèmes à base de cortisone » can only appear as a customer quote, after a legal check.
  • Supplement wording. Lines about digestion or the gut are health claims for food supplements and need a check.
  • Permission. Confirm the review terms cover use in ads, or ask the customers we quote.
